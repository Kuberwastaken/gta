Previews name five apps on Jason and Lucia's phone: Snapmatic for social media, BuckMe for banking, Waink for sizing up cars, RydeMe for ride-hailing and Scooter Bros for vehicle delivery <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. The rule that matters most: the two leads can't post publicly on Snapmatic, because they're fugitives. They can follow accounts, watch the feed and DM photos to each other.

## What apps are on the GTA 6 phone?

| App | What it does | Grade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Snapmatic | Instagram/TikTok parody; follow accounts, watch NPC events and livestreams in real time | <span class="tag-reported">reported</span> |
| BuckMe | Holds your bank balance | <span class="tag-reported">reported</span> |
| Waink | Scans a car's security tier, tracker, registration cost and resale value | <span class="tag-reported">reported</span> |
| RydeMe | Uber parody; passengers chat | <span class="tag-reported">reported</span> |
| Scooter Bros | Delivers a vehicle to you for a fee | <span class="tag-reported">reported</span> |

Sources are Red Bull, Engadget, VICE and Edge (via GTAVice.net). Only Snapmatic and RydeMe are explicitly described as parodies of named real apps. LifeInvader and Bleeter, social networks from earlier games, have not been confirmed <span class="tag-rumour">rumour</span>.

## Can you post on social media in GTA 6?

No public posts. Snapmatic is a window onto Leonida rather than a megaphone: you follow accounts and watch NPC events and livestreams as they happen in the world, while Jason and Lucia are limited to sending photos to each other by DM <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. An optional livestream feed opens with R3, which outlets read as a nod to LifeInvader <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. Rockstar hasn't said whether Snapmatic reacts to crimes you commit or whether a camera mode goes beyond DMs.

## How does social media figure in the story?

Rockstar treats influencer culture as a core target, and its official bios show it <span class="tag-confirmed">confirmed</span>. The [[characters/real-dimez|Real Dimez]] turned shaking down local dealers into money "via spicy rap tracks and a relentless social media presence", and Dazed reports their arc "can be traced via in-game viral social media posts" <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. [[characters/cal-hampton|Cal Hampton]] spends his evenings "snooping on Coast Guard comms with a few beers and some private browser tabs open."

Trailer 1 set the tone by recreating real Florida viral clips, such as the alligator wandering into a convenience store <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. More on [[media/gta-6-easter-eggs-in-trailers|trailer easter eggs]].

## What do BuckMe and Waink do?

BuckMe is the safe place for money. Cash on you is lost when you're Wasted or Busted; the bank balance in BuckMe is not <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. The three money types are explained on [[gameplay/gta-6-money-banks-and-fences|money, banks and fences]].

Waink answers whether a car is worth stealing before you touch it: its security tier, whether a tracker will alert police, what registration costs and what it would resell for <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. See [[gameplay/gta-6-vehicles|vehicles]] and [[gameplay/gta-6-wanted-system-and-heat|the wanted system]].

## Can you play the radio on your phone?

Yes. Rockstar's music hub says radio and podcasts can be heard on foot through the phone, a series first <span class="tag-confirmed">confirmed</span>. Edge counts 23 stations in total <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>; the six Rockstar has revealed are on [[music/gta-6-radio-stations|radio stations]].
