Rockstar has officially named **seven GTA 6 vehicles**, all on its edition and pre-order pages: the **'95 Grotti Cheetah, '55 Vapid Stanier, Vapid Ganado, Dinka Enduro, Crest Kayak, Shitzu Squalo and '67 Vapid Dominator Buggy**, plus a gold donk from Rideout Customs <span class="tag-confirmed">confirmed</span>. Everything else comes from press reports of Rockstar screenshots or from fans identifying cars in trailers.

The systems around them are the bigger story: security tiers for theft, fuel, trunk storage, garages and a phone app that scans cars before you steal them <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>.

## How does stealing cars work in GTA 6?

Per Red Bull, Edge and VICE <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>:

- **Security tiers.** Vehicles have different levels of protection.
- **Tools.** Hot-wiring, key cloners or duplicators, lock picks, smashing a window (which triggers the alarm), or a quick-time-event hijack.
- **Trackers.** Some cars carry trackers that alert police. See [[gameplay/gta-6-wanted-system-and-heat|the wanted system]].
- **Registration.** Stolen cars can be registered for a fee.
- **Waink app.** Scans a car's security, tracker, registration cost and resale value. More on [[gameplay/gta-6-phone-and-social-media|phone apps]].

## Is there fuel in GTA 6?

Yes, per previews <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. Fuel gauges return and you refuel by holding the button for about 10 seconds. Engine health is tracked too.

## What real cars are GTA 6 brands based on?

By fan convention: Grotti for Ferrari, Pegassi for Lamborghini or Pagani, Vapid for Ford, Declasse for Chevrolet and Bravado for Dodge <span class="tag-rumour">rumour</span>. Rockstar has never confirmed real-world equivalents. More on Leonida's real-world inspirations at [[world/gta-6-real-life-locations|real-life locations]].
