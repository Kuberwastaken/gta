GTA 6 keeps a Criminal Profile for each lead: a meter running from Professional at one end to Irredeemable at the other, marked with angel and devil icons. Edge's October 29 cover feature and Red Bull's gameplay roundup both report that it affects the story <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. New November 25 interviews add developer explanations below. They do not supply a complete scoring table or exact thresholds.

## How does the Criminal Profile work?

Because it is per character, [[characters/jason-duval|Jason]] and [[characters/lucia-caminos|Lucia]] can end up with different reputations depending on how you play each. You switch between them when the story allows; see [[gameplay/gta-6-character-switching|character switching]].

## Is it the same as the wanted level?

No. The wanted level is the police in the moment: six stars, witnesses, CCTV, your clothes and your car <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. The Criminal Profile is the long-run record of what kind of criminal each lead is. Whether one feeds the other is not on the record. Police rules are on [[gameplay/gta-6-wanted-system-and-heat|the wanted system and heat]].

## What does the November 25 developer interview add?

[Eurogamer's Chris Tapsell publishes a direct pre-launch interview with Rob Nelson](https://www.eurogamer.net/gta-6-empathy-criminal-profile-why-rockstar-added-it-rob-nelson) explaining why Rockstar developed the system. Nelson describes early thinking in **2015 and 2016**, while much of the studio was still working on Red Dead Redemption 2: more detailed NPC reactions and choices beyond shooting. His example is threatening someone during a robbery and giving them a chance to comply, rather than requiring lethal violence. <span class="tag-reported">reported</span> (developer interview)

Nelson describes smaller optional choices alongside the story, sometimes involving extra effort or a sacrifice by the player. He connects those choices to bringing open-world behavior closer to the character being portrayed. That is design reasoning, not a promise that every threat succeeds, every NPC reacts identically or any specific action awards a known number of points. See [[gameplay/gta-6-weapons|combat and weapons]] and [[gameplay/gta-6-money-banks-and-fences|robberies and money]] for separately sourced mechanics.

### What does Irredeemable mean?

Nelson says repeated rampages can push the profile into **Irredeemable**, which he describes as a way for players who want chaos to opt out of the system. He contrasts that intent with repeatedly greeting people to recover low honor in RDR2. He also says **profile notifications can be turned off**. <span class="tag-reported">reported</span> (developer statement)

These are different things: hiding notifications is not the same as disabling tracking or entering Irredeemable. Eurogamer does not provide an exact threshold, a recovery route, a reset command or proof that every related consequence disappears. We have not tested those limits. Its writer also questions whether the system rewards behavior or simply labels it; that interpretation should not become a verified bonus schedule.

## Does it change the ending?

The earlier pre-launch uncertainty is now outdated. In [Critical Hits' November 25 Portuguese-language interview with Nelson](https://criticalhits.com.br/games/rob-nelson-da-rockstar-fala-a-critical-hits-sobre-o-tom-de-gta-6-os-dois-protagonistas-o-ritmo-da-campanha-e-os-finais/), the profile is named alongside the relationship, savings and decisions as factors in the result. <span class="tag-reported">reported</span> (English paraphrase of the published interview)

That is not a complete formula or a claim that one meter alone decides the result. This guide leaves out outcome descriptions, counts, requirements and story examples. The [[characters/jason-and-lucia-relationship|relationship guide]] covers that system separately. Neither interview announces a new patch or a post-launch change to these mechanics.

## How does it fit the characters?

Rockstar's bios set up both ends of the meter. Lucia has "learned her lesson - only smart moves from here"; Jason "wants an easy life, but things just keep getting harder" <span class="tag-confirmed">confirmed</span>. A meter measuring how far each drifts from those intentions fits the writing, though Rockstar has not drawn the connection <span class="tag-speculation">speculation</span>.

Still unresolved here: exact point values, tier thresholds, recovery limits and a complete list of mission effects.
