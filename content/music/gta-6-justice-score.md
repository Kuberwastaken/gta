Justice, the French duo of Gaspard Augé and Xavier de Rosnay, wrote GTA 6's original score. The album, *Grand Theft Auto VI: Original Score*, comes out December 11, 2026 on streaming, CD and vinyl with 30 tracks plus digital bonus tracks, per Rockstar <span class="tag-confirmed">confirmed</span>. The score itself plays in the game from the [[release/gta-6-release-date|November 19 launch]]; only the album waits three weeks.

## When was Justice announced for GTA 6?

On October 15, 2026, with the [[media/gta-6-launch-trailer|Launch Trailer]], which premiered the Justice track "Flower in a Hurricane" featuring Tame Impala, SZA and King Gizzard & The Lizard Wizard <span class="tag-confirmed">confirmed</span>. NME and Game Rant covered the reveal the same day. Two weeks later, on October 29, the score album was dated for December 11 and a second single, "Pusher" featuring Gesaffelstein, came out, per Wikipedia <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>. On November 22, Rockstar's music page lists three singles, adding Starting from Zero featuring Michael McDonald to the two previously tracked here; the score album date is still December 11.

## What is an adaptive score?

Music that responds to play rather than running as a fixed track. Rockstar's description is "expansive compositions all dynamically tuned to the different ways players engage with the world" <span class="tag-confirmed">confirmed</span>, and it bills Justice as "Grammy Award winners". That is the official product-page description; the November 25 developer interview below now adds an account of how the technology changed. Neither source gives a complete trigger map or explains every interaction with the [[music/gta-6-radio-stations|radio]] mix in a car.

The interviews below separate the technology behind the adaptive score from the composers' production process and the album release.

## How does GTA VI adapt the score during play?

**November 25 developer account:** in [Final Weapon's pre-launch Q&A, published by Noah Hunter](https://finalweapon.net/2026/11/25/gta-6-interview-rockstar-games-world-design-music-combat/), **Alastair MacGregor, vice president of audio**, says personalization was the goal for music accompanying both the story and the open world. He names collaboration with **Justice and Overmono**. <span class="tag-reported">reported</span> (developer interview)

MacGregor says Rockstar replaced its traditional **multi-stem system**, which limited harmonic progression and song structure, with a **non-linear approach to composition, asset production and playback**. In his account, the game sequences, mixes and processes the score in real time in response to player choices and actions. This adds technical context to the older description of music being dynamically tuned to play.

## What did Justice say about making the score?

In [Billboard's November 23 direct interview](https://www.billboard.com/music/features/gta-6-radio-songs-soundtrack-justice-billboard-cover-1236386805/), **Xavier de Rosnay** describes scoring portions of the game and revisiting them as Rockstar rewrote or moved material. He says the score needed to cover different places, situations and people rather than settling on one central sound. <span class="tag-reported">reported</span> (composer interview)

Rockstar's references included Justice's earlier work, especially *Audio, Video, Disco*, and a direction to keep a sense of fun. Justice describe drawing on their own musical style and their view of Miami through 1980s screen culture. That is a production account, not evidence that every location has a fixed musical genre or that the score has a player-selectable mode.

The feature discusses collaboration with **Gesaffelstein** and **Michael McDonald**, already named on the official singles. It also describes the use of song stems in some gameplay sequences. We leave out which sequences and where individual songs appear. Neither that reporting nor Rockstar's adaptive-score description specifies every gameplay trigger, crossfade rule or runtime behavior.

### Is the score 30 tracks or 50 pieces?

Billboard describes the score as **50 pieces of music**. Rockstar's current product page still says **30 tracks plus a number of digital bonus tracks**, with the album due **December 11**. Keep the reporting and product wording separate: the interview does not supply a complete 50-row release list or establish that every CD and vinyl has 50 tracks. We have not counted unreleased files or verified an exact digital runtime.

## Why does the score come out after the game?

Rockstar hasn't said. The original Album was announced for November 19 and reported released November 20; the score album follows on December 11. One search summary in our research applied the December 11 date to the radio stations. That is wrong: the stations are in the game at launch, and December 11 is the score album only <span class="tag-debunked">debunked</span>.
