Lucia Caminos is GTA's first non-optional female protagonist and the first woman to lead a 3D-era mainline GTA. She is not the first woman you could play as: the original Grand Theft Auto (1997) and GTA 2 on Game Boy Color let you pick female characters, and GTA Online lets you build a female avatar.

The wording we use: Lucia is "the first female protagonist of a 3D-era mainline GTA, and the series' first non-optional female lead."

Dazed's full line: Lucia is "the first fully fleshed-out female lead in a mainline GTA game. Before her, it's male protagonists all the way down, dating back to 1999." Starting the count at 1999 skips the 1997 original, which Dazed notes "featured a 50-50 split of male and female protagonists."

## Which GTA games had playable women before Lucia?

Wikipedia's GTA VI article footnotes three:

- Grand Theft Auto (1997), the top-down original, with a selectable roster Dazed describes as split 50-50 by gender.
- GTA 2 on Game Boy Color, which offered selectable female characters.
- GTA Online, whose custom avatars can be female.

None had a written story arc. They were avatars, which is why "first non-optional" is the accurate phrase.

The Chinatown Wars version is <span class="tag-debunked">debunked</span>. GTA: Chinatown Wars (Nintendo DS, 2009) starred Huang Lee, per GTABase. There was no female lead to follow.

GamesRadar+ ran the Bloomberg report under the line that GTA 6 would "feature series' first female protagonist," alongside reporting on Rockstar overhauling its workplace culture. Most of it held: Lucia and [[characters/jason-duval|Jason]] are "a Bonnie and Clyde-type narrative" in the words of Rockstar narrative SVP Rupert Humphries, and the setting is [[world/vice-city|Vice City]], Rockstar's Miami. Rockstar's bio does not state Lucia's ethnicity, so "Latina" remains Bloomberg's reporting.

## Is Lucia a co-lead or the main character?

Co-lead. Rob Nelson told Famitsu: "This game is the story of Jason and Lucia." Rockstar considered four protagonists and rejected the idea because more leads would mean less time with each. Dazed calls GTA 6 "the first time that a GTA game has featured a playable couple in the lead role." The dynamic is on the [[characters/jason-and-lucia-relationship|relationship page]]; control handoffs are in [[gameplay/gta-6-character-switching|character switching]].

Rockstar's bio gives her a specific past: her father "taught her to fight as soon as she could walk," fighting for her family put her in Leonida Penitentiary, and her family came from Liberty City. More on the [[characters/lucia-caminos|Lucia Caminos]] page. PC Gamer's Christopher Livingston wrote that [[media/gta-6-extended-look|Extended Look]] audiences favored her for her "creative takedowns." <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>

## Who wrote Lucia?

Rockstar has not published writing credits. Wikipedia credits the Extended Look to Rupert Humphries and Michael Wiafe. <span class="tag-reported">reported</span>

Dan Houser, co-founder and lead writer from GTA III through Red Dead Redemption 2, left Rockstar on March 11, 2020. He has said GTA VI is "not going to be a story I wrote," which makes it the first mainline GTA story since 1997 without him. See the [[timeline/gta-6-development-history|development history]].
